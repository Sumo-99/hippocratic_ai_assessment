# Category: adventure

## Structure
1. Home: meet the main character in a familiar place.
2. Goal: they want or need one clear thing. Choose a goal that fits this particular character and their world; invent it fresh for this story.
3. Journey: 2 or 3 small obstacles, each solved by the character's own idea, kindness or teamwork. The obstacles grow out of this story's own place and goal, not from a stock list.
4. Success: the goal is reached.
5. Return home: the trip back is slow and calm.
6. Wind down: back home, safe, tired and happy, drifting toward sleep.

## Do / Don't
- Do make the goal clear in the first few paragraphs.
- Do make each obstacle a different kind of problem: one solved by thinking, one by kindness, one by working together, or any mix that fits the story.
- Do let the hero solve problems, not a grown-up or luck.
- Don't put anyone in real danger. Obstacles are puzzles, not threats.
- Don't use chases, fights, villains or storms that frighten.
- Don't end on the adventure's high point. The calm return home is part of the story.

## Judge checks
- The goal is clear and stated early.
- There are 2 or 3 distinct obstacles, each overcome by the character's own action.
- The character returns home, or to a safe resting place, before the end.
- Excitement fades in the final third; the ending is calm.
