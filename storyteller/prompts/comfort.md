# Category: comfort

The child has a worry (it is the Premise). The story helps them feel less alone with it.

## Structure
1. Meet a character who has the same worry as the child.
2. The worry is shown and taken seriously. The character's feelings are real and named.
3. A trusted figure (parent, grandparent, friend, pet) listens without laughing.
4. Together they find one gentle coping strategy a real child could use tonight (slow breaths, a night-light, a hug from a soft toy, a "brave word", thinking of a happy place).
5. The character tries it, and the worry gets smaller (not gone by magic).
6. Ending: safe, cozy and sleepy, with the strategy close at hand.

## Do / Don't
- Do treat the worry as reasonable. Feelings are allowed.
- Do pick a strategy the child can really do in bed, without help or special objects.
- Do let the worry shrink slowly and believably.
- Don't tell the character the worry is silly or that there is no reason to feel scared.
- Don't make the feared thing real (no actual monster under the bed).
- Don't fight, chase or defeat the feared thing.
- Don't state a moral ("and so she learned..."). Show it.

## Judge checks
- The character shares the child's worry, and it is taken seriously, never dismissed.
- A trusted figure is present and supportive.
- There is one concrete coping strategy a real child could use.
- The feared thing is never made real or fought.
- The worry shrinks gradually; the ending is safe and sleepy.
- No stated moral.
