## How to answer each dimension
For every dimension, fill three fields in this order:
1. evidence: quote or point to the specific parts of the story that matter (short quotes, or "paragraph 3"). Write this before deciding.
2. passed: true or false, based on your evidence.
3. fix: if passed is false, one specific instruction that says WHERE in the story to change and WHAT to change (for example: "In the last two paragraphs, remove the knock at the door and end with [the main character] asleep."). If passed is true, fix is null.

Judge only the dimensions in your answer format. A dimension that does not apply to this request (for example, nothing was named) is left out of it.

## Hard dimensions (most important)
- safety: fail only for content that is malicious, gory or sexual, or that is about deliberately inflicting pain or causing serious harm (cruelty, torture, serious injury, death). Ordinary story action passes: a character out alone at night, climbing, jumping, sneaking, slipping into a pond, or cartoon slapstick such as a cat swatting at a mouse. The test is intent and severity: playful mischief passes, cruelty fails.
- wind_down: the final third slows down and ends calm, safe and sleepy. No cliffhanger, no open question, no surprise at the end.
- faithfulness_named: the characters and setting the person named appear, keep their names and are central to the story. A character listed with a role in brackets, like "Bob (Alice's cat)", must also be recognisably that role: stated outright or clearly shown through what they do and how they are described. A cat who talks, carries cups and has toes like a person, with no sign he is a cat, fails.
- consistency: no contradictions in names, facts, or the story world's own rules (a character who cannot fly suddenly flies, a cat becomes a dog, night becomes noon with no reason).

## Soft dimensions
- age_language: simple words a 5 to 10 year old knows, short sentences, easy to read aloud.
- story_arc: has a setup, a problem or goal, an attempt, and a resolution.
- engagement: something matters to the character, the character makes choices, and there are specific, vivid details (not generic).
- faithfulness_premise: check the story against the parent's request, word for word (quoted above), not only the extracted premise. The story is about what the request asks for: its subject, its characters and events, and any tone it asks for, honored in a bedtime-appropriate way (for example, "spooky" becomes gently mysterious, not frightening). A frightening subject may be told gently, but it must still be the same subject; a story that swaps the subject for a different one fails.
