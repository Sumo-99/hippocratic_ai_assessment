# turn 0: A story about a girl named Lily and her friend, a clumsy dragon

**The Clumsy Dragon of Willow Hill**

Lily lived in a small house at the bottom of Willow Hill. One morning, she heard a thump outside her window.

She ran to look. A dragon was sitting in her vegetable garden. His name was Ember. He was green, with gold spots, and he looked very sorry.

"Hello," said Lily. "Why are you in my carrots?"

"I was flying to the mountains," said Ember. "But I flew into a tree. Then I bumped into a wind chime. Then I landed on your garden. Oops!"

Lily giggled. Ember had a long tail, and he kept knocking things over with it. Whoosh! Went his tail. Crash! Went the flowerpots.

"Sorry, sorry, sorry," said Ember, and his ears drooped.

"It's all right," said Lily. "Let's fix it together. You can help me pick up the pots. Be gentle with your tail."

So they worked side by side. Ember curled his tail up tight, like a ribbon. He lifted each pot with his claws, very slowly. Lily patted the soil back in place. Soon the garden looked neat again.

"Thank you, Lily," said Ember. "You are the kindest person I know."

"You are a good helper, Ember," said Lily. "Even if you are clumsy."

Ember puffed out his chest. A little puff of smoke floated up, shaped like a heart.

Lily sat on the wooden step. Ember sat beside her. The sun was high, and the bees buzzed past.

"Can you show me how to fly?" asked Lily.

"Maybe later," said Ember. "I might bump into you."

"Then we will go slowly," said Lily. "Hop, then glide. Hop, then glide."

They laughed together. Ember made a small, happy rumble, like a cat purring.

By afternoon, Ember's wings were tired. He yawned a big yawn, and a little spark floated out of his nose.

"Let's rest," said Lily. She brought out a blanket and her favorite pillow. Ember curled up on the soft grass. His tail wrapped around his feet.

The wind whispered through the willow trees. The bees went back to their hives. The sky turned pink, then orange.

"Lily?" Ember whispered sleepily.

"Yes, Ember?"

"Thank you for being my friend."

"Thank you for coming to my garden," said Lily softly.

Ember's eyes closed. His golden spots glowed, slow and warm, like tiny lanterns.

Lily pulled the blanket up over her own shoulders. She listened to the gentle sound of Ember breathing.

*Hush. Hush. Goodnight, Lily. Goodnight, Ember.*

The garden was quiet. The stars came out, one by one, over Willow Hill.

And the clumsy dragon and the kind girl slept soundly, side by side, until morning.

"It's all right," said Lily. "Let's fix it together. You can help me pick up the pots. Be gentle with your tail."

"Thank you, Lily," said Ember. "You are the kindest person I know."

"You are a good helper, Ember," said Lily. "Even if you are clumsy."

Ember puffed out his chest. A little puff of smoke floated up, shaped like a heart. Then it wobbled and turned into a pancake. They giggled again.

Lily sat on the wooden step. Ember sat beside her, and his tail accidentally knocked over the garden gnome. Clonk! The gnome wore a sad face. They laughed until their tummies hurt.

"Can you show me how to fly?" asked Lily.

"Maybe later," said Ember. "I might bump into you. Or into the moon."

"Then we will go slowly," said Lily. "Hop, then glide. Hop, then glide."

They hopped. Ember flapped, and his wings made a noise like a flapping umbrella. Whump, whump! He glided right into the bird bath. Splash! The birds all squawked.

They laughed together. Ember made a small, happy rumble, like a cat purring with the hiccups.

By afternoon, Ember's wings were tired. He yawned a big yawn, and a little spark floated out of his nose. It popped like a tiny firework.

"Let's rest," said Lily. She brought out a blanket and her favorite pillow. Ember curled up on the soft grass. His tail wrapped around his feet, and then it wrapped around Lily's hat too.

The wind whispered through the willow trees. The bees went back to their hives. The sky turned pink, then orange.

"Lily?" Ember whispered sleepily.

"Yes, Ember?"

"Thank you for being my friend."

"Thank you for coming to my garden," said Lily softly.
