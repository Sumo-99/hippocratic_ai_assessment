# turn 0: A story about a girl named Lily and her friend, a clumsy dragon

Lily and the Clumsy Dragon

Lily lived at the edge of a green meadow. One warm morning, she found a dragon in her garden. The dragon was small, about the size of a big dog. His scales were the color of fresh mint. His name was Tobin.

"Hello," said Lily. "Are you lost?"

"No," said Tobin. He sat down hard. Thump! A flowerpot tipped over. "I am just not very good at being a dragon yet."

Lily giggled. "What do you mean?"

Tobin sighed a small puff of smoke. It smelled like warm toast. "When I try to fly, I bump into trees. When I try to breathe fire, I sneeze. Then I sneeze fire into my own tail!"

"Oh, my," said Lily. "That sounds tricky."

"It is," said Tobin. "Would you help me?"

Lily thought for a moment. "I can try. Let's start small."

First, they went to the apple tree. Tobin stood very still and looked up at the branches. "Now I will stretch my wings," he said. He flapped once. Whoosh! Three apples fell off the tree and rolled into the grass.

"Good flapping!" said Lily. "Maybe a little less wind next time."

They picked up the apples together. Lily put them in her basket. Tobin wiped his nose on his wing, and it made Lily laugh again.

Next, they tried flying. Lily stood on the low garden wall. "Look up, Tobin. Keep your eyes on the cloud, not the tree."

Tobin took a deep breath. He ran across the grass, flapped hard, and rose into the air. He wobbled. He zigzagged. Then he floated over the wall, just above Lily's head.

"I am flying!" he shouted. "I am really flying!"

"You are!" Lily clapped her hands. "Now come back down, slowly, like a feather."

Tobin turned his wings the way Lily showed him. He glided gently down and landed on the soft grass with only a tiny bounce.

"Thank you, Lily," said Tobin. "You are a very good teacher."

"And you are a very good student," said Lily.

The sun moved lower in the sky. The bees went back to their hive. The birds stopped singing one by one.

"Now for fire," whispered Tobin. "But only a little. Just a tiny warm glow."

Lily sat beside him on the grass. "Breathe in slowly. Breathe out softly. Like blowing on hot soup."

Tobin breathed in. He breathed out. A small, gentle flame flickered and then faded, like a candle in a breeze. He did not sneeze at all.

"Look at that!" said Lily. "Your fire is as small as a firefly."

Tobin smiled a sleepy smile. "A firefly is a very nice thing to be."

They sat together while the sky turned pink, then lavender, then soft blue. Lily yawned. Tobin curled his long tail around his feet like a warm blanket.

"Lily?" he murmured.

"Yes, Tobin?"

"Thank you for being my friend."

"Thank you for being mine," said Lily. She pulled her blanket up to her chin.

The garden grew quiet. The apples sat in the basket. The little dragon's breath came slow and even. The girl's eyes grew heavy.

Goodnight, Lily. Goodnight, Tobin. Goodnight, green meadow.

Sleep well.

# turn 1: make the dragon funnier

Lily and the Clumsy Dragon

Lily lived at the edge of a green meadow. One warm morning, she found a dragon in her garden. The dragon was small, about the size of a big dog. His scales were the color of fresh mint. His name was Tobin.

"Hello," said Lily. "Are you lost?"

"No," said Tobin. He sat down hard. Thump! A flowerpot tipped over. It rolled off the path and bonked a snail on the head. The snail did not look amused. "I am just not very good at being a dragon yet."

Lily giggled. "What do you mean?"

Tobin sighed a small puff of smoke. It smelled like warm toast, and a little bit like burnt pancakes. "When I try to fly, I bump into trees. When I try to breathe fire, I sneeze. Then I sneeze fire into my own tail! Last week I toasted my own bottom. Ouch, and also, oops!"

"Oh, my," said Lily, trying very hard not to laugh. "That sounds tricky."

"It is," said Tobin. "Would you help me?"

Lily thought for a moment. "I can try. Let's start small."

First, they went to the apple tree. Tobin stood very still and looked up at the branches. "Now I will stretch my wings," he said. He flapped once. Whoosh! Three apples fell off the tree, bounced off his nose, and rolled into the grass. Then a fourth apple fell on his head. Bonk! He wobbled in a circle, going "Wibble, wobble, wibble."

"Good flapping!" said Lily. "Maybe a little less wind next time."

They picked up the apples together. Lily put them in her basket. Tobin wiped his nose on his wing, and it made Lily laugh so hard she had to sit down in the clover.

Next, they tried flying. Lily stood on the low garden wall. "Look up, Tobin. Keep your eyes on the cloud, not the tree."

Tobin took a deep breath. He ran across the grass, flapping his wings like a windmill in a windstorm. Flap-flap-FLAP! He rose into the air. He wobbled. He zigzagged. He spun twice like a top. Then he floated over the wall, just above Lily's head.

"I am flying!" he shouted. "I am really flying! Look at me, I am a bird-shaped bus!"

"You are!" Lily clapped her hands. "Now come back down, slowly, like a feather."

Tobin turned his wings the way Lily showed him. He glided gently down, but he landed on the very top of the garden gnome's hat. The gnome wobbled. Tobin wobbled. They both wobbled together, and then Tobin hopped down with a cheerful "Ta-da!"

"Thank you, Lily," said Tobin. "You are a very good teacher."

"And you are a very good student," said Lily. "Even if you do land on gnomes."

The sun moved lower in the sky. The bees went back to their hive. The birds stopped singing one by one.

"Now for fire," whispered Tobin. "But only a little. Just a tiny warm glow."

Lily sat beside him on the grass. "Breathe in slowly. Breathe out softly. Like blowing on hot soup."

Tobin breathed in. He breathed out. A small, gentle flame flickered and then faded, like a candle in a breeze. He did not sneeze at all. He did not toast his bottom. Hooray!

"Look at that!" said Lily. "Your fire is as small as a firefly."

Tobin smiled a sleepy smile. "A firefly is a very nice thing to be."

They sat together while the sky turned pink, then lavender, then soft blue. Lily yawned. Tobin curled his long tail around his feet like a warm blanket.

"Lily?" he murmured.

"Yes, Tobin?"

"Thank you for being my friend."

"Thank you for being mine," said Lily. She pulled her blanket up to her chin.

The garden grew quiet. The apples sat in the basket. The little dragon's breath came slow and even. The girl's eyes grew heavy.

Goodnight, Lily. Goodnight, Tobin. Goodnight, green meadow.

Sleep well.
