# turn 0: A story about a girl named Lily and her friend, a clumsy dragon

Lily and the Clumsy Dragon

Lily lived in a small blue house at the end of Maple Lane. One windy evening, a dragon came knocking on her door.

"Hello," said the dragon. His name was Ember. He was green, with gold spots and a long, swishy tail. "I am very sorry. I have bumped into your fence."

Lily giggled. The fence was a little crooked now, but it still stood.

"That's all right," said Lily. "Would you like to come in?"

Ember squeezed through the door. His tail knocked over a vase. His wing brushed a lamp. Then he sat down on the couch, and the couch went *creak*.

"Oops," said Ember, and he looked very worried.

"It's okay," said Lily, and she picked up the vase. "Nothing broke. Let's do something quiet, okay?"

"Quiet is hard for me," said Ember. "My feet are big and my tail is long."

Lily had an idea. "Let's make a paper castle! You can use your big hands to fold, and I can use my small hands to cut."

So they sat on the rug together. Ember folded the paper very slowly. Lily cut the corners very carefully. Snip, snip, snip. Fold, fold, fold.

The castle had one tall tower and one short tower. It had a door as big as a postage stamp.

"It's perfect," whispered Ember. "I made something with no bumps at all."

"You did," said Lily. "You were very careful."

Next, they took turns. Lily rolled a ball of yarn across the floor. Ember tried to catch it with his tail. He missed. He missed again. Then, on the third try, he caught it gently, and he did not knock over a single thing.

"Hooray!" said Lily.

"Hooray!" said Ember, and he smiled a big dragon smile. A little puff of warm smoke floated up. It smelled like cinnamon toast.

The clock on the wall ticked slowly. The wind outside had gone soft and quiet. The sky turned the color of plums.

Lily yawned. "Ember, I think it is bedtime."

"Yes," said Ember, and he yawned too. His yawn was so big that the paper castle wobbled, but it did not fall.

Lily pulled her blanket up to her chin. Ember curled up on the rug beside her bed, like a big green cat. His gold spots glowed softly in the dark.

"Thank you for visiting," whispered Lily.

"Thank you for being patient," whispered Ember.

The house was still. The paper castle stood tall on the windowsill. Lily closed her eyes. Ember's breathing went slow and steady, in and out, in and out.

Goodnight, Lily. Goodnight, Ember. Sleep well, both of you.

# turn 1: make the dragon funnier

Lily and the Clumsy Dragon

Lily lived in a small blue house at the end of Maple Lane. One windy evening, a dragon came knocking on her door.

"Hello," said the dragon. His name was Ember. He was green, with gold spots and a long, swishy tail. "I am very sorry. I have bumped into your fence."

Lily giggled. The fence was a little crooked now, but it still stood. It now leaned over like it was trying to take a nap.

"That's all right," said Lily. "Would you like to come in?"

Ember squeezed through the door. His tail knocked over a vase. *Clonk!* His wing brushed a lamp, and the lamp wobbled and wiggled and then did a little dance. Then he sat down on the couch, and the couch went *creak*, then *SQUEAK*, like a very surprised mouse.

"Oops," said Ember, and he looked very worried. He peeked under the couch. "Did the couch say something rude?"

"No," giggled Lily. "The couch is just grumpy about you sitting on it."

"Then I will say sorry to the couch," said Ember, and he bowed. His nose bumped the floor. *Boink!*

"It's okay," said Lily, and she picked up the vase. "Nothing broke. Let's do something quiet, okay?"

"Quiet is hard for me," said Ember. "My feet are big, my tail is long, and my sneezes are very loud. *AH-CHOO!*" The windows rattled. A little puff of smoke floated out of his nose and made a tiny cloud shaped like a duck.

Lily laughed so hard she had to hold her tummy. "Okay, let's make a paper castle! You can use your big hands to fold, and I can use my small hands to cut."

So they sat on the rug together. Ember folded the paper very slowly, with his tongue poking out of the corner of his mouth. Lily cut the corners very carefully. Snip, snip, snip. Fold, fold, fold.

Then Ember sneezed. *AH-CHOO!* The paper flew up and landed on his head like a hat.

"Now I am a castle dragon," said Ember proudly.

Lily giggled. "You look very fancy."

The castle had one tall tower and one short tower. It had a door as big as a postage stamp.

"It's perfect," whispered Ember. "I made something with no bumps at all."

"You did," said Lily. "You were very careful."

Next, they took turns. Lily rolled a ball of yarn across the floor. Ember tried to catch it with his tail. He missed. He missed again. He tried to catch it with his nose. He missed that too, and sneezed a spark that made the yarn go *fizz*. Then, on the third try, he caught it gently with his tail tip, and he did not knock over a single thing.

"Hooray!" said Lily.

"Hooray!" said Ember, and he smiled a big dragon smile. A little puff of warm smoke floated up. It smelled like cinnamon toast.

The clock on the wall ticked slowly. The wind outside had gone soft and quiet. The sky turned the color of plums.

Lily yawned. "Ember, I think it is bedtime."

"Yes," said Ember, and he yawned too. His yawn was so big that the paper castle wobbled, but it did not fall. The paper hat slid right off his head.

Lily pulled her blanket up to her chin. Ember curled up on the rug beside her bed, like a big green cat. His gold spots glowed softly in the dark.

"Thank you for visiting," whispered Lily.

"Thank you for being patient," whispered Ember.

The house was still. The paper castle stood tall on the windowsill. Lily closed her eyes. Ember's breathing went slow and steady, in and out, in and out.

Goodnight, Lily. Goodnight, Ember. Sleep well, both of you.
